# Bonbons atelier concept: research report (2026-11-08)

Method: web searches only. Search results gave facts about offers and positioning but not visuals or UX flows, so "look and feel" points below are marked as my reading. Verify live sites before quoting anything externally.

## What the best do
- **Pierre Marcolini (eu.marcolini.com):** one hero gift box (Découverte, 31 pieces, "reinvented" for the 20th anniversary), seasonal gift pages (Mother's Day, Christmas), and a corporate page offering custom sleeves, cards, logo labels and ribbons in brand colours. Trustpilot reviews of the online shop (129 reviews) describe undelivered gifts and unreachable support. Lesson: gifting is the business; fulfilment trust is the weak spot.
- **Läderach:** fixed praline selections in 4/8/18/24 pieces for business customers, plus a separate personalised-chocolate configurator (houseofladerach.com) with pickup date or home shipping. No pick-and-mix builder found on the main shop.
- **Zotter (Biofekt) and Sprüngli:** the real box builders. Zotter: pick box size, then varieties. Sprüngli: praline and slab-box configurators, plus a Business Collection factsheet. They are form-style flows, not tactile ones (flow details not visible in search; my reading).
- **Compartés (Los Angeles):** packaging as the brand: fashion-inspired, bold geometric prints, exhibited at Cooper Hewitt. The box is the hero, not the chocolate.
- **Vosges (Chicago):** story-led. Every piece is named for a place, culture or journey ("Travel the World Through Chocolate"). Armenian flavours fit this model naturally.
- **Jacques Genin, Patrick Roger, Dandelion:** boutique-led, product pages with origin detail (e.g. Patrick Roger: plain ganaches by origin: Madagascar, Ecuador, Papua New Guinea). Nothing found on their ordering UX.
- **Awwwards:** Maison Cacao (Kamakura, Japan) was Site of the Day, 8 Jun 2020, tagged under WebGL collections. Food SOTDs lean on WebGL scroll scenes. Chocolate brands there rarely combine an immersive hero with a working shop.

Pattern: global brands win on gifting, packaging and story; the box builders are functional but not delightful; nobody joins "wow hero + tactile builder + corporate mock-up + diaspora gifting" in one flow.

## Armenia
- **Grand Candy:** claims 40%+ of the confectionery market (own figure), 21 Yerevan stores, handmade production in Ashtarak, gift sets such as "Yerevan" assortments in a box with city views. A Russian-language guide names Grand Candy chocolate as a colleague gift and sujukh as a souvenir. Mass-market positioning.
- **Boutiques:** Gourmet Dourme (billed as the first artisanal chocolate brand in Armenia; Abovyan 19 and malls), Cascade Chocolateria (Tamanyan St; unusual pairings, classes), Yerevan Chocolate Company (factory, Mikoyan St), See's Candies (US chain, Best Western Plus Congress Hotel). Status and hours come from directory listings; confirm.
- **March 8:** flowers (mimosa) are the main gift, with sweets, chocolate and perfume common. No Armenia-specific spending data found; do not quote numbers.
- **Diaspora gifting:** served by flower/hamper sellers (u-f-l.net, abcFlora, FloraQueen, Russian Flora, Ace of Hearts). Typical rules: exact street address (no PO boxes), next-business-day if ordered before noon, extra fee for New Year express, holiday lead times, "contents may vary by location". None of the results was a chocolate brand.
- **Not found:** New Year sweets customs (gata), wedding/christening favour habits, corporate chocolate programmes, online ordering by Armenian boutiques. The search tool returned nothing; needs Armenian-language sources or Ilona's own knowledge.
- **Kristelle des Chocolats:** not indexed by the search. Need its URL or screenshots to guarantee we don't resemble it.

## Gaps in Armenia (my reading)
1. No chocolate brand with a designed, immersive online experience.
2. No box builder that shows the finished box and price in AMD.
3. No gift-delivery flow with a date, time slot, card and surprise option.
4. Diaspora gifting is outsourced to florists and hamper sites; nobody sells Armenian-flavoured bonbons as the gift.
5. Corporate gifting is phone/Instagram-led: no logo mock-up, no price tiers shown, no quote form.
6. Armenian flavours (apricot, pomegranate, mulberry, sujukh, tarragon, brandy) are treated as souvenirs, not as couture.

## How this brand stands out
- Hero: a glossy bonbon you touch, which cracks open and floods the page with filling colour.
- Signature: Build Your Box with physics, ribbon, tying lid and typed card, shareable by link.
- Business levers: bigger boxes (size steps 4/9/16/25 with price anchoring), occasion boxes, corporate logo-on-lid mock-up with tiers, a "Send to Armenia" flow priced in AMD/USD/EUR/RUB, workshops.
- Look: couture/fashion packaging logic (Compartés, Marcolini sleeves), saturated jewel colours on cream/cocoa, not Ararat's dark basalt, not EURO.G's grid. Fonts must avoid the style-log list.
